I am in Peter Café. This is the place in Horta, Isla Faial en los Azores,
that has welcomed sailors for over one hundred years. Joshua Slocum passed
through here at the beginning of his solo circumnavegation. Uruguayan sailor
Eduardo Rejduch writes about finding this place at the end of his first
passage, in 1981, when he sailed from New York City using a pilot chart and
a toy sextant.

A sextant. I still don't know how to do sun sights. I had my own toy sextant
for a while. I don't know what I did with it.

The visit of Eduardo, he writes that it
was something notable. They put it in the local paper. 
Now the harbor is crowded with sailboats.
Everybody is doing it. Mostly it is people with money. It has become an
industry. Everywhere you go there are facilities to check you in and
charge you and ask for your insurance papers.
Where the money goes, little kingdoms grow to take some of it.

I imagine that Horta is a great deal different, now. There is no beach with
boats pulled up onto it.  There are no fishermen working on their nets.

There has been a festival here, [Maravilha][maravilha]. The waterfront park
has stages for music performances. A woman constructed carousels for the
children using bamboo poles and rope, hung from a tree on a single purchase.

[maravilha]: https://festivalmaravilha.pt/

To the east, across the Faial Passage is the island of Pico. It has been
shrouded in cloud most of the time. My first morning I poked my head out
of the cabin and there was a mountain. I thought, "where did that come from!"
