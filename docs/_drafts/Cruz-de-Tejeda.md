I rode a bus up to the town of San Mateo, here on the island of Gran Canaria.
I poked around the very empty town, made some pictures, stopped in a shop,
visited the café in which the few inhabitants of the town seemed to be
congregating. From there I took another bus to Cruz de Tejeda. It is an
overlook. The cross is a stone sculpture crucifix.  It was also nearly entirely
vacant. Perhaps Tuesday is a holiday.  I bought some dried figs and a bottle of
water from a vendor. "A good price," he said. "A good price for him," I
thought. A small thing. There are so few people. The man has to eat.  I have
some good figs.

I made a picture of the volcano, Teide, 3700m high above the ocean. One
hundrend and fifty kilometers distant, it is a small pyramid nearly lost in the
blue of the sky.  Yet there it is, there across more ocean, on the island of
Tenerife.  This altitude, the altitude of Cruz de Tejeda, 1500m, gave me a
headache.

The main event was the ride on the bus. Leaving the city, going up into the
hills, it feels like Los Angeles. Then, the narrow, two lane, unpainted road
carved into the sides of the hills winds its way up along the valleys,
making switchbacks. The sides of the valleys are terraced for buildings,
small towns and farm fields. A farm field is an acre if that. A big one
is an acre.

There wasn't much going on up there. It is pretty rural. It felt apart from
the world. Never mind that it was sky high.
