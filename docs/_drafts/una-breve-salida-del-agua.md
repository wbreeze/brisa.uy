Before going back to sea, before sailing to Cabo Verde, before recrossing the
atlantic from east to west, before crossing the doldrums, the ITCZ, before
crossing the equator from the northern to the southern atlantic ocean, from
the northern hemisphere to the southern hemisphere, before entering the
southern trades and making landfall in Brazil to make the final, coastal
journey to Uruguay, before all of that, with all of that ahead of me, I decided
it would be best to tend to one of the places where water can enter the boat,
one I haven't tended to for a while.

After leaving the boat in a boatyard in the Bahamas during my last trip to
Uruguay, when launching the boat again in March, I noticed a good trickle
of water entering through the seal around the propeller shaft. I thought, oh
that. I might have to attend to that right away.

Watching it, it stopped as the material soaked in the water and expanded.
All was well. Still. It was in the back of my head to replace the old
packing with new.

The material is wax impregnated woven flax. There are also synthetics. Over
time the wax leaches out. Heat from friction and the rubbing make the flax
less supple, a little burned. I think the time to replace it has more to do
with the amount of use, running under power, than it does with time. It's a
little of both.

So I did this. It's a chore in a hard to reach place. Now it is done and I
have more peace of mind. Leaving the marina, while underway, I adjusted the
tension of the packing nut so that it ran cool, dripping once every ten
seconds or so. If the nut is too tight the friction makes it run hot. Hot
means more wear on the packing material. It won't last long. Properly adjusted
it runs cool, water cooled, with a drip underway and no drip when the
propeller is idle, still.

There was one mistake with the haul-out. I allowed them to pressure-wash the
hull below the waterline. This is pretty standard practice. Haul it. Wash it.
It didn't occur to me not to do it. They put it in the quote and I approved it.

The guy who did it was very aggressive. He held the pressure wand close to the
hull, almost touching, only a few centimeters away. I watched in pain as
hundreds of dollars of ablative bottom paint went flowing down the drain.
The water ran off of the hull like a red tide.
I had applied three coats a year ago when I hauled-out in Maryland. Good thing,
because the second just went down the drain here in Tenerife. I'm down to the
first coat. It has to last the year, or at least until arrival in Piriapolis.

This blue paint that I applied to the topsides in Panama keeps failing to
grip. The waves beating on the hull wear it off. Paint masking tape pulls it
off. Fenders against a dock and the bands from the lift wear it off. No matter
the preparation-- careful sanding and cleaning --it peels away. If there's a
next time I'll use something better. One advantage is that it can be applied
using a roller, not sprayed. I sanded the damaged patches and repainted them
for about the sixth time.

One new thing. I applied a magic spell, eyes on the bow to see across the sea.
I've been wanting to do this for a long time. While seated next to another
sailor, at a barbeque for a friend at Club Nautilus, doodling on a pad of paper
with paint brush and watercolor, the design came to me.  Here it is. Eyes on
the prize. Brisa has sight to guide me home.
